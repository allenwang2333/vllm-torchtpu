## 0. What R2 was asked, and what it had to substitute

The brief pointed at `hlo_stats` "the same pull as `j-a71a648c`, different config." That pull is not
reproducible as written. All four 2026-09-02 GLM-5.2 captures (`j-7afc895e`, `j-97102390`,
`j-a71a648c`, `j-f37c33fb`) are the **AgentX InferenceX workload**; two of the four contain only DP
lockstep dummy batches and are unusable. **No nightly-configuration in-model profile exists**, and
producing one needs an end-to-end model benchmark, which R2 forbids.

So R2 reproduces the *table*, not the *job*: `hlo_stats` over a single-call microbenchmark of
`streamindex_topk` at the nightly shape, on the 4-chip pod. This is legitimate for exactly this
question — `hlo_stats` reports **per-instruction device self time**, which carries no host dispatch
floor, and the residual question is a question about device work. It is *not* legitimate for
anything about cross-layer scheduling or stall, and §5 says where that bites.

Four arms, two of them controls:

| arm | config | batch | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nightly` | `max_model_len` 9216, page 1024, `num_positions` 10240 | 64 | the answer |
| `nightly_rep` | same, independent chip | 64 | fan-out contention control |
| `nightly16` | nightly config | 16 | separates *batch* from *config* |
| `agentx` | `max_model_len` 262144, page 256, `num_positions` 262144 | 16 | the comparison point |

`nightly16` exists because `report.md` §6.2 was retracted for moving two axes at once. Here they
are separated.

**Provenance of the code under test.** The image at `ec4bcecf` predates both kernel modules, so they
are `--map-dir`'d in and the shard *reads back* what it imported:
`streamindex_topk.py md5=1e221f10cbf3573aa0d2ae78c3463532`,
`sparsecore_topk.py md5=8bc9e25b8701a53a97704564b9fbc658`. The first matches P3's recorded md5 and
`git diff --stat cbabaf9b` over the kernels directory is empty, so this is byte-identical to what
baseline job `j-8b1e7acc` ran. Geometry read back matches `bench_prod.json` exactly
(`page_size=1024 num_positions=10240 num_tokens=64 score_buffer=2.50 MiB kv_cache=144.00 MiB
enable_early_exit=True`).

**Contention.** `nightly` and `nightly_rep` ran on separate chips of the same contended pod and
agree to **0.02%** (170.474 vs 170.437 µs/call). Device self time is not wall clock; the ±8.5%
fan-out spread that governs every other probe in this exercise does not apply to this instrument.

## 1. The ~60 µs re-derived — and corrected to 27 µs

The coordinator's derivation was `221.4 − 110.38 (P4, stage A) − ~50 (P2/P3, stage B) − ~1 (init)
≈ 60 µs`. Reproducing it exactly with the tightest available terms:

    221.39  bench total, prod_decode_b64_ctx8704      (j-8b1e7acc, wall clock)
  − 110.38  P4 sbs4_grid16                            (different job, wall clock)
  −   1.10  two empty kernel launches                 (P4, wall clock)
  −  48.45  P2 prodrows stage B                       (different job, wall clock)
  −   0.94  init, modelled at 2.62 MB / 2.79 TB/s
  = 60.52 µs

So the ~60 reproduces. **It is nonetheless not a device-work residual.** `total` is one dispatched
program and carries one host dispatch floor; the three subtrahends are three separately dispatched
programs carrying three. Subtracting them re-adds ~2 floors.

Measured directly instead, from one `hlo_stats` table of one program in one job:

| | µs/call | source |
|---|---:|---|
| TensorCore self time, whole call | **170.474** | measured, this job |
| host dispatch floor at this shape | 42.005 | `bench_prod.json`, `dispatch_floor_ms` |
| sum | 212.48 | |
| bench wall clock | 221.39 | `bench_prod.json`, `total_ms` |
| unaccounted (inter-op gaps, final sync) | 8.91 | |

and the residual is

    170.474  TensorCore self time, whole call
  − 111.688  stage A (three StreamIdxTC custom-calls)
  −  31.807  stage B on the TensorCore critical path
  =  26.979 µs

**The genuine device residual at nightly is 26.98 µs, not 60.** The 33.5 µs difference is 0.80
dispatch floors — i.e. the cross-job subtraction's known bias, showing up at the expected size.

### Overlap discipline

The brief warns that an earlier analysis subtracted two disjoint buckets. Two checks:

1. **The three terms above are a verified partition**, not three independent measurements. Every
   TensorCore row of the table is assigned to exactly one bucket and the assignment is asserted:
   `sum(buckets) − TC total = 0.000000` for all four arms (42, 42, 80 and 99 rows, all untruncated —
   `rows_kept == rows_total` was checked explicitly after a first dump silently truncated at
   `--top-n 60`).
2. **The SparseCore-plane row is excluded, not added.** `sc_topk_direct…call-done` appears on both
   planes: 30.978 µs/call on SparseCore (reported at 0.00% of module self time) and 31.675 µs/call
   on TensorCore (15.28%). They are the *same asynchronous operation*. Only the TensorCore charge —
   what the critical path actually pays — is counted.

## 2. The composition of the 26.98 µs, itemized

Nightly, per call (= per layer; the microbenchmark's one call maps 1:1 onto one indexer layer —
its 2 `StreamIdxTC-d` and 1 `StreamIdxTC-m` instructions are exactly the per-layer multiplicity the
brief establishes as 42 and 21 across 21 layers).

| item | nightly µs | AgentX µs | Option D |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `cond.5.clone` — the `conditional` control op itself | **6.591** | 7.240 | **removed** |
| metadata C2/C5 — token→seq maps, `_effective_row_lengths`, `distribution` slices | **8.172** | 6.761 | **amortized 21→1** |
| stage-B data shaping — `bitcast_convert_type` of the 2.5 MiB f32 score matrix (3.476), output `reshape` (1.160) | 4.637 | 82.541 | keep |
| scalar prefetch C3 — `start_end_seq_idx` (1.231), `sem_ids` (0.588), `bo_sz` (0.480) | **2.298** | 2.292 | **amortized 21→1** |
| `broadcast.13.scores_in_hbm_ref` — the `-inf` init | 1.655 | 6.067 | keep |
| indexer-weights layout `copy` + bf16→f32 `convert` | 1.452 | 1.297 | keep |
| `reduce_max.7` — the `cond` predicate `jnp.max(seq_lens)` | **1.330** | 1.287 | **removed** |
| `copy-done.4` — jit-boundary copy of the s32[64,2048] result out of `S(1)` | 0.844 | 26.798 | keep (see below) |
| other (AgentX only: `slice-done`, `broadcast_bitcast_fusion`) | 0.000 | 6.256 | keep |
| **residual total** | **26.979** | **140.539** | |

*Every figure in the "nightly µs" column is measured device self time from `j-7cd67df2` at the
nightly configuration. Every figure in the "AgentX µs" column is measured device self time from the
same job at the AgentX configuration and **batch 16**, which is a microbenchmark at the AgentX
shape — it is not the in-model AgentX profile and must not be read as one.*

The `agentx` column's three large "keep" entries (`gather_offload_async_done` 55.0,
`bitcast_convert_type.6` 12.1, `copy.9` 23.5) are `sparsecore_topk` taking a *different code path*
at that config — a multi-wave gather-offload rather than nightly's single direct call. That is a
real structural difference between the two deployments, not noise, and it is why the AgentX
residual is 5× the nightly one while the metadata inside it is not.

### The 44.8 µs is dead, measured

`report.md` §6 defines the `init` column as "the `jnp.full(-inf)` fill alone", separately measured
as its own jitted arm; §1.1 of the design doc relabels it a residual. It is not one, and its
magnitude is a dispatch floor: `init_ms` 44.83 against `dispatch_floor_ms` 42.01, ratio 1.067.
**Measured in-program at nightly, the same fill is `broadcast.13.scores_in_hbm_ref` = 1.655 µs** —
27× smaller than the number Option D's magnitude argument rested on. Confirmed non-hoistable by the
HLO: the kernel carries `output_to_operand_aliasing={{}: (10, {})}`, so the `-inf` buffer is donated
into the kernel's output and each layer needs its own.

### Three routes agree on stage A

`hlo_stats` gives stage A's main call at **109.869 µs**, against P4's independently measured
`sbs4_grid16` = 110.38 µs (different job, different method — 0.5% apart) and `report.md` §6's
derived 109.2 µs (1.1% apart). The substitution in §0 is corroborated where it can be checked.

### Microbenchmark boundary artifacts

About 2.25 µs of the 26.98 (`copy.5` 0.836, `convert_element_type.8` 0.617, `copy-done.4` 0.794)
exists because `streamindex_topk` is the *entry* computation here and would not appear in this form
in-model. All three are in the "keep" set, so they inflate the residual without touching the Option D
estimate. `copy-done.4` is specifically the copy of the conditional's `S(1)` result into the default
space; it would plausibly disappear with the conditional, and is **not** credited to D.

## 3. What Option D can remove, and what it cannot

Option D replaces the runtime `lax.cond` with a trace-time Python `if` on the static
`max_model_len`, and hoists layer-invariant metadata above it. Three distinct effects:

**(a) The conditional and its predicate go outright — 7.921 µs/layer.** After D there is no
`conditional` instruction, so `cond.5.clone` (6.591) and `reduce_max.7` (1.330) have nothing to
attribute time to. That `cond.5.clone` costs 6.6 µs of *self* time — excluding both branch bodies —
is the direct measurement of the mechanism §3.3 describes: the HLO shows it taking a 7-element
tuple `%cond.3` spanning the 144 MiB `cache_kv` and the 2 MiB `q`, and carrying a 32 MiB
`scoped_memory_configs` annotation. It is near-invariant across all three configurations
(6.591 / 6.649 / 7.240) — fixed control-flow cost, not data-dependent.

**(b) 10.470 µs/layer of layer-invariant metadata is amortized 21→1, saving 20/21 = 9.972 µs.**
Not 21/21: the work still has to happen once per forward pass. `seq_lens`, `cu_q_lens`,
`distribution` and `block_tables` are the same tensors for all 21 indexer layers, so the hoist is
valid; the 21× multiplicity is the brief's established structural fact (86 `region_*_spmd`, 84
`conditional`s, every metadata row an exact multiple of 21), which R2 takes rather than re-measures.

**This metadata is on the critical path by data dependence and cannot be hidden.** The HLO shows
`%broadcast.15` (`sem_ids`), `%broadcast.14` (`bo_sz`), `%broadcast.13` (init) and the scalar-prefetch
tuple elements as **direct operands** of `%StreamIdxTC-d-bq_1-bkvp_2.2`. They are strictly upstream
of the kernel they feed; no scheduler can overlap them with it.

**(c) 8.588 µs/layer it cannot touch, with reasons:**

- **stage-B data shaping, 4.637** — `bitcast_convert_type` reinterprets the *whole 2.5 MiB f32 score
  matrix* as s32 for the radix select, and `reshape` reshapes the s32[131072] output. Both consume
  this layer's data. Hoisting has no purchase on either.
- **the `-inf` init, 1.655** — donated per-layer output buffer (above).
- **indexer-weights layout copy + convert, 1.452** — each layer has its own weights. Note 0.836 µs to
  copy a 4 KiB bf16[64,32] is essentially pure fixed cost, but it is per-layer data, so a hoist
  cannot share it. (A dtype/layout change upstream could; that is not Option D.)
- **jit-boundary copy, 0.844** — an artifact of the microbenchmark, deliberately not credited.

## 4. The pinned number

| | µs/layer | % of 170.47 µs device | % of 221.4 µs wall | µs/step/core (×21) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| low | 10.03 | −5.9% | −4.5% | 211 |
| **central** | **17.83** | **−10.5%** | **−8.1%** | **374** |
| high | 18.39 | −10.8% | −8.3% | 386 |

**Modelled.** Composed from measured per-instruction device self time at the nightly configuration
(`j-7cd67df2`), times a 21× layer multiplicity established structurally rather than measured at this
configuration. It is not a measured speedup and no run of Option D exists.

Central = 6.591 (conditional, 21/21) + 1.330 × 20/21 (predicate) + 10.470 × 20/21 (metadata).
Low assumes the conditional's 6.591 µs is operand marshalling and scoped-memory setup that
reappears as explicit copies once the branch is inlined, *and* that `reduce_max.7` — which sits
outside the branch and so is CSE-eligible — already collapses across the 21 layers in-model, neither
of which this single-call microbenchmark can rule out. High credits everything at 21/21.

**Which denominator.** 221.4 µs is the design doc's baseline of record, but it is a microbenchmark
wall clock containing a ~42 µs host dispatch floor that does not exist in-model, where all 21 layers
are inside one XLA program. **−10.5% of device time is the physically meaningful figure for the
model; −8.1% is what a re-run of the microbenchmark would show.** Report the one you mean.

The design doc's central −8.2% came from `0.5 × 44.8 × 0.81`, an arithmetic over a quantity that
§2 shows is 27× too large. That it lands 0.1 pp from the microbenchmark-denominator number here is
two errors cancelling. It is not corroboration and should not be cited as such.

### Cross-check against the in-model AgentX profile

The one place the microbenchmark substitution can be checked against a real in-model measurement is
the bucket D targets. The in-model AgentX profile reports **225.8 µs/step/core of scalar plumbing**
within 265.7 µs/step/core of metadata scopes. This job's AgentX-config microbenchmark measures the
Option-D-reachable metadata at 9.052 µs/call × 21 = **190.1 µs/step/core**. Agreement to 16%, with
the microbenchmark on the *low* side — so the estimate above is, if anything, conservative.

## 5. Does the AgentX→nightly shrinkage apply? No.

Elsewhere in this exercise AgentX→nightly shrank a quantity 10× (5.4 → 0.55 µs/layer), and the
brief names the resulting uncertainty as the reason Option D's range was uselessly wide. **It does
not apply to this bucket.** Measured, Option-D-reachable per call:

| arm | conditional + predicate | metadata (C2/C3/C5) | reachable | ×21 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `nightly` (b64, nightly cfg) | 7.921 | 10.470 | **18.391** | 386.2 |
| `nightly_rep` (replicate) | 7.933 | 10.481 | 18.414 | 386.7 |
| `nightly16` (b16, nightly cfg) | 7.976 | 12.345 | 20.320 | 426.7 |
| `agentx` (b16, AgentX cfg) | 8.527 | 9.052 | 17.579 | 369.2 |

AgentX → nightly is **1.05×, in the direction of nightly being *larger***. Reading the two controls
one axis at a time:

- **batch** (nightly b64 → b16, config held): 18.39 → 20.32, a 1.10× *increase*.
- **configuration** (b16, nightly → AgentX: `max_model_len` 9216→262144, page 1024→256): 20.32 →
  17.58, a 1.16× decrease.

Neither axis moves it more than ~15%, across a **16× change in `max_model_len` and a 4× change in
batch**. The reason is visible in the itemization: this bucket is scalar and tiny-vector work —
three s32 gathers, a couple of slices, an iota-compare, two constant broadcasts — whose cost is
per-instruction issue overhead. It does not scale with sequence length, which is the axis the 10×
shrinkage rode on. Compare the same table's stage A, which does: 111.69 (b64) → 31.67 (b16).

**This is R2's main result.** The `−0% to −20%` range existed because the residual's composition was
only known at AgentX and might have shrunk 10×. It is now measured at nightly directly, *and* shown
to be shape-invariant, so the range collapses from either direction.

## 6. Two caveats that bound what this says, and one process note

**The critical-path caveat.** These are device *self times*; they say what work exists, not what the
TensorCore was blocked on. The in-model AgentX profile showed 174.6 µs/layer of TensorCore **stall**
alongside 125.25 µs of compute. Metadata removed from stall time saves nothing. §3(b) argues
structurally that this particular bucket cannot be in stall time — the HLO makes it a direct operand
chain feeding the kernels — but the argument is from data dependence, not from a nightly in-model
schedule, and no nightly in-model profile exists to close it. Producing one requires an end-to-end
benchmark. **R2 stops here and says so rather than launching one.**

**The 21× caveat.** The multiplicity is taken from the brief's structural facts and from the AgentX
profile, not measured at nightly. The in-model AgentX profile counts 84 `conditional`s against 21
indexer layers — 4 per layer, where `streamindex_topk` contributes 1. The extra three are elsewhere
in the model and are not claimed by Option D; the multiplier used here is 21, not 84.

**Process note for the write-up.** The `hlo` step ran green on the first job
`j-adddca06-330d-4631-9c57` — `JobStatus=Succeeded` — while all four shards had rc=1 with
`TypeError: streamindex_topk() got an unexpected keyword argument 'enable_early_exit'` and empty
output directories, because the image's own `src` shadows the mapped-in kernels on `PYTHONPATH`.
The fix was the `--map-dir` plus the md5 read-back in `r2_shard.sh` §ENV, so that a mapping which
silently did not take can no longer be mistaken for a valid measurement. Also, for the record:
baseline job `j-8b1e7acc` was itself a four-shard fan-out on one 4-chip pod, so its absolute
microseconds carry the same contention spread as the probes' — the design doc implies only wave 2
was contended, and that is wrong.
