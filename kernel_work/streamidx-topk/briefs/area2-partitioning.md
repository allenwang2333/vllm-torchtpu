# Area (2) — Partitioning — constraint sheet for the step-2 designer

## A. Constraints

1. **The grid is already real-request-sized; there is no bucket-padding waste to reclaim at the grid level.** `grid = ((end_seq_idx - start_seq_idx)//seq_batch_size,)` with both ends traced from `distribution`, which the runner sets to the real count. `source (streamindex_topk.py:643, mla_attention_op.py:183)`. Nightly decode, 64 seqs → grid 16. A design that "skips padding slots" is proposing something that already happens.
2. **Within a grid step the partition is unguarded and constant-length.** `_fetch_bkv` is a Python loop over `seq_batch_size` emitting `bkv_p` unconditional `page_size_per_kv_packing`-row DMAs with a *clamped* (not predicated) page index; the trip count is the single scalar `kv_len_max = max(kv_lens)`. `source (:168-194, :275-276)`. Every slot in a group of 4 pays the group max, and the last bkv block is always full width.
3. **Therefore the reference's ragged-tail discount does not apply.** `02-partitioning.md` measures a partial tail at **0.65 of a full step**, explicitly because *the tail DMA shrinks while the arithmetic does not*. Here the tail DMA does **not** shrink. `from 02-partitioning.md` + `source (:177-194)`. Cost the incumbent's tail at **1.0**, not 0.65.
4. **At homogeneous nightly decode the rounding waste alone is 15.0%**, before any length spread: `num_bkv=cdiv(8704,2048)=5`, 5×2048=10240 positions computed per slot against 8704 useful. `modelled` from `source`. Length spread is *additional* and unquantified — no artifact in this repo has a served batch's length distribution.
5. **`bkv_p` is not free to raise: it widens `num_sublanes_total`, which is stage B's row width.** `num_sublanes_total = max_p align_to(pages_per_seq,bkv_p)*page_size//128`, `source (:603-605)`; and stage B cost tracks buffer width (5.9× for 25.6× at byte-identical KV, `measured (j-c13c8436-ee1e-49ee-b514)`). At `pages_per_seq=9`: bkv_p=2→10240, **bkv_p=3→9216**, bkv_p=4→12288, bkv_p=8→16384. **bkv_p=3 is strictly better on paper on every axis** — 10% narrower score buffer, `num_bkv` 5→3, KV bytes 167.8→151.0 MB — and no code change beyond the tuple. `modelled`. Flag: this is cheap enough that it must be measured before anything else is designed, or a later win will be measured against a baseline that was leaving it on the table.
   > **⚠️ Amended by `## Correction after §6.3` at the end of this file.** The score-width half of this constraint is now much weaker; the recommendation itself survives on the other two halves. Read the correction before acting on this item.
6. **VMEM is nowhere near binding, in either call.** Decode scratch: bkv 4.00 MiB + bq 32 KiB + weights 1 KiB + score 64 KiB = **4.09 MiB**; prefill scratch = **4.03 MiB**. No operand is pipelined (all `pltpu.HBM`), so scratch *is* the resident set. `source (:654-692)`. Against the 31 MiB default scoped ceiling `from 02-partitioning.md`. The incumbent has ~7× headroom it does not use.
7. **Except that prefill's block shape implies a 32.0 MiB f32 intermediate.** `compute_scores` einsum at `bq_sz=128` is `(1, 128*32=4096, 2048)` f32 = **33.55 MB**, at/over the 31 MiB default ceiling, which is why `DEFAULT_VMEM_LIMIT_BYTES = 100 MiB` is set. `source (:28, :326-331)`. **This is an alternative hypothesis for prefill's 857 µs that §4 attributes to an `assumed` VPU rate the §7 probe failed to measure.** Whether Mosaic tiles it or materialises it is `[unverified]` and is the prefill probe I would run before the VMEM-resident VPU probe.
8. **100 MiB is not a budget.** Physical VMEM is 64 MiB and the reference's warning applies verbatim: *"a design that only fits above the default limit has no headroom left for the next operand."* `from 02-partitioning.md`. Any candidate must state its footprint against 31 MiB and separately against 64 MiB.
9. **Traversal order at prefill is bq-outer / bkv-inner, and both loops are independent** — no accumulator crosses them (the head reduction is inside one `compute_scores` call). `source (:418, :420)`. So the KV re-read `num_bq`=8× (21.0 MB, 14% of prefill traffic, `modelled`) is recoverable by **swapping the two `fori_loop`s**, at the cost of re-reading q 5× — q is 4 MB fp8 total. Legal, and the reference's 7% traversal result is a *lower* bound here because that experiment's re-read was of an f32 accumulator, not of the dominant operand.
10. **No causal block-skipping exists.** `num_bkv` is computed once from `kv_len_max` outside the bq loop; every bq block scores every bkv block and masks. `source (:276, :340-352)`. At the §6 prefill shapes (last chunk of a long context) this costs <10%; it dominates only when the chunk is a large fraction of the context, which the early-exit path already catches at ctx≤2048.
11. **Three `pallas_call`s are launched per op invocation, and at a pure-decode step two of them get a zero-length grid.** `distribution = [rd0, rd0, rd2]`, so at 64 decode seqs the grids are 16 / 0 / 0. `source (:755-809, mla_attention_op.py:178-183)`. `measured (j-a71a648c-3064-471a-a131)`: 5.40 µs and 5.22 µs self-time each → **21 layers × 10.6 µs ≈ 223 µs/step of launching kernels that do nothing.** This is the report's own candidate explanation for the `StreamIdxTC-d` bimodality and I believe it. Note these are AgentX numbers; the *count* is deployment-independent, the µs is not.
12. **Merging those three calls is constrained: `seq_batch_size` and `bq_sz` are static** — they size the VMEM scratch shapes. `source (:654-684)`. One call cannot vary them per grid step; merging means paying prefill's scratch on every decode step. Merging calls 1 and 2 only (decode batched + remainder) is possible via masking inside the batched kernel, and is the cheaper half.
13. **Batching across sequences is decode-only:** `if seq_batch_size > 1: assert static_q_len == 1`. `source (:628-629)`. Not binding at the nightly config (no MTP), binding if speculative decoding is turned on.
14. **Stage B's partition is already exactly full at nightly and has no lever.** `_pick_partition(64, 10240, 2048)` → p=1 (single stage, no `gather_offload`); `num_waves = cdiv(64,32) = 2`; 32 subcores × 2 waves = 64 rows, **0% subcore idle**. `source (sparsecore_topk.py:360-377, :303, :148)`, hardware `measured (j-0b008512-36f2-4eb2-bdc6)`: 2 cores × 16 subcores. (At AgentX b=16 → 1 wave, 16/32 subcores → 50% idle. Not the target.)
15. **Stage B skips empty rows but not short ones.** `i_hi = 0` when `eff_len = 0` gates the DMA away entirely — token-bucket padding rows in stage B are free. But a non-empty row DMAs `slice_len = n` words regardless of `row_lengths`; only the scan loops are `i_hi`-bounded. `source (sparsecore_topk.py:155-166)`. So the answer to the report's step-2 question "can stage B's row width be bounded by `seq_len`?" is: **the scan already is; the HBM read is not**, and the read is 2.62 MB/call at nightly, 64 MB at AgentX.
16. **The score buffer already uses the rank-3 `(tokens, sublanes, 128)` shape** that `02-partitioning.md` names as the legal way to carry a trailing extent that is not a native tile. `source (:726)`. Any redesign must keep the last two dims 8/128-divisible or equal to the array extent.
17. **Grid step count is not a cost to optimize.** 26 ns/step `from 02-partitioning.md`; the nightly decode grid is 16 steps. **Prefill's grid is 1 step** — there is no cross-step pipelining at all, and all overlap comes from the hand-rolled inner double-buffering. `source (:643)`.
18. **`dimension_semantics=("arbitrary",)` is correct and must stay.** The prologue/epilogue and the `sem_ids_ref` / `bo_sz_ref` SMEM scratch carry state across grid steps. `source (:424-435, :721)`. `parallel` here would be a correctness bug, and on tpu7x (`is_megacore=False`) it buys nothing anyway.
19. **Sorting sequences by length (the incumbent's own TODO at `:751-754`) has one nameable partitioning cost:** `start_send_scores` currently issues **one** DMA covering `seq_batch_size*sz` *contiguous* token rows. `source (:137-151)`. Under a permutation the 4 rows are no longer contiguous, so that becomes 4 DMAs + 4 waits. Bytes and stride are unchanged (the destination row stride is already 40 KiB), so the cost is 3 extra semaphore round trips per bkv step, not extra traffic. The permutation itself is an SMEM indirection array (≤64 × i32 = 256 B) — area (7)'s to place.

## B. `Emit …` schema — fields, with what each means here

Fill one instance **per `run_scores_kernel` call site** (decode-batched / decode-remainder / mixed) plus one for stage B. Do not aggregate them; they have different static parameters.

- **`grid`** — the tuple, its extent *as a function of `distribution`* (it is traced, not static), and `dimension_semantics` per axis with the state that justifies `arbitrary`. Say explicitly what the extent is at nightly decode (64 seqs) and at nightly prefill (1 seq): the incumbent is `(16,)`, `(0,)`, `(0,)` and `(0,)`, `(0,)`, `(1,)`.
- **`inner iteration space`** — the two `fori_loop` bounds and their order, since they are not part of the grid and are where all the real work is. `num_bq = cdiv(static_q_len, bq_sz)`, `num_bkv = cdiv(kv_len_max, bkv_sz)`. State which is outer and the operand each ordering re-reads (constraint 9).
- **`block shapes`** — `seq_batch_size`, `bq_sz`, `bkv_p` → `bkv_sz = bkv_p × page_size`, and the derived `num_sublanes_total = align_to(pages_per_seq, bkv_p) × page_size / 128`. **`num_sublanes_total` is an output of the bkv choice and an input to stage B's cost — carry it explicitly (constraint 5).**
- **`working set vs capacity`** — sum the four VMEM scratch buffers *with arithmetic shown*, plus the einsum intermediate `(seq_batch_size, bq_sz×num_q_heads, bkv_sz)` f32, against **31 MiB (default scoped) and 64 MiB (physical)**. Do not quote the 100 MiB `vmem_limit_bytes` as capacity. Note that nothing is pipelined by Pallas, so there is no ×2 double-buffering multiplier on operands — the ×2 is already written into the scratch shapes.
- **`kernel invocations per step`** — count all three TC launches whether or not their grid is empty, then ×21 indexer layers, then the SparseCore launches (1 at nightly, 3 + a `take_along_axis` at AgentX). Nightly decode incumbent: **63 TC launches/step, 42 of them empty; 21 SC launches; 336 working grid steps; 13,440 KV DMAs of 256 KiB.**
- **`total HBM traffic implied`** — five lines, not one: (a) K-cache stream `= Σ_groups seq_batch_size × num_bkv × bkv_sz × lkv_dim`; (b) q + weights; (c) kernel score write-back; (d) the `jnp.full(-inf)` init (`T × num_sublanes_total × 128 × 4`, `measured (j-a71a648c)` at 6.02 µs in-model — real but closed); (e) stage B's full-width read `T × num_positions × 4` + its `T × k × 4` write. Incumbent nightly decode: 167.8 / ~0.3 / 2.62 / 2.62 / 3.14 MB ≈ **176 MB**.
- **`idle fraction`** — report it as five separate numbers, because they have different fixes and only the first two are area (2)'s:
  - *bkv rounding*: `1 − kv_len_i / (num_bkv × bkv_sz)` = **15.0%** at homogeneous nightly (fix: predicate the tail);
  - *within-group length spread*: `1 − mean(kv_len)/max(kv_len)` over each group of `decode_req_batch_size` = **0% at homogeneous, unknown at real batches** (fix: sort, or predicate per slot);
  - *empty launches*: 42/63 TC launches, ≈223 µs/step at the only rates measured (fix: merge calls, or `lax.cond`);
  - *causal*: <10% at the §6 prefill shapes (fix: bound `num_bkv` per bq block);
  - *stage B subcores*: 0% at b=64 (nothing to fix).
  Cross-reference but do not double-count area (4)'s byte-level 129/256 = 50.4%; multiplied through, only **42.8%** of streamed decode K-cache bytes are useful.

## C. Gaps in `02-partitioning.md` this block turns on

- **"Whether VMEM bytes-per-element tracks itemsize below 4 bytes is `[unverified]`"** — bf16 and int8 hit no boundary up to a 1024 MiB request, which means the scratch was elided and the probe measured nothing. **Turns on.** The `bkv_x2` buffer is **uint8** and is 97% of the decode scratch. Decision: if uint8 really is 1 B/element, `bkv_p=8` (16.0 MiB) fits under the default 31 MiB ceiling with no `vmem_limit_bytes` at all; if narrow dtypes are charged at 4 B/element or the ceiling behaves differently for them, `bkv_p=8` needs 64 MiB and is off the table, and `bkv_p=4` (8 MiB nominal) becomes the largest safe step. Every "make the KV tile bigger" candidate branches on this, and it is a one-shot bisect.
- **"The block sweep does not extend past 1024 on any axis, so the shape at which VMEM turns the monotone trend around was never found."** **Turns on.** Both einsums here sit outside the sweep's box: decode is M=32 (a quarter of the sweep's smallest M, and below the 128 MXU row floor the file cites), prefill is M=4096 and N=2048 (both at or past the top). Decision: whether raising `bkv_p` (N: 2048→4096→8192) is still monotonically better, which is the sole performance argument for raising it against constraint 5's score-width penalty. Without a data point above N=1024 the trade is undecidable and the designer should default to **`bkv_p=3`** — the one setting that improves *both* sides — rather than betting on extrapolation.
- **"*Why* the ragged tail is 0.65 of a step rather than 1.0 has not been isolated"**, with the plausible reading being that the DMA shrinks and the arithmetic does not. **Turns on, and the answer is already partly known here.** Decision: whether predicating the tail bkv block is worth writing. If the 0.65 is the DMA, then the incumbent — whose tail DMA is constant-length (constraint 2) — pays 1.0 and predicating recovers the full 15% of 167.8 MB ≈ 25 MB on the term that is 95% of decode traffic. If the 0.65 is something else, predicating recovers only the compute, which the roofline says is not what binds decode. This is the cheapest discriminating probe in the area.
- **Not turned on:** `dimension_semantics` (constraint 18 — one axis, correctly labelled, no Megacore); the tokamax-deflates-`k`-last disagreement (there is no contraction axis to deflate: K = `head_dim` = 128, fixed by the model and already at the file's floor); the corrected-source-pointers note.
- **A gap the file does not have and should:** it prices a grid *step* (26 ns) and a *host* dispatch (~150 µs) but nothing for an **in-model `pallas_call` whose dynamic grid extent is 0**. That is 42 of this block's 63 launches per step. The only figure anywhere is 5.40 µs from `j-a71a648c` — AgentX, and inferred rather than isolated. It belongs in the file once measured.

## D. Where `02-partitioning.md` and `report.md` contradict

1. **The dispatch floor, and the ~785 µs validity rule.** The reference: *"any measurement of a region under ~785 µs is measuring the host"*, with a 150 µs per-`pallas_call` floor. Taken at face value this invalidates **every number in report §6** — the largest is 929.9 µs and the decode headline is 221.4 µs. The report reaches the opposite conclusion and *retracts* a 43 µs dispatch add-back as fitting. **I believe the report, and the two are reconcilable rather than contradictory:** the reference's own text scopes the 150 µs to "a single `block_until_ready`", and `bench_streamidx_topk.py`'s `time_arm` enqueues `iters` calls and blocks once, so the round trip amortises. The in-model profile settles it independently — the `-inf` init that the bench arm reads at 28.6 µs is **6.02 µs** in the compiled program (`measured, j-a71a648c`). **Designer consequence:** do not use 150 µs to price the empty launches of constraint 11, and do not use it to argue that a candidate's microbenchmark delta is noise. Use 5.40 µs and the report's own ±8.5% run-to-run spread, and re-measure the incumbent in the same job as every challenger.
2. **Ragged-tail cost.** The reference's 0.65-of-a-step is a *measurement of a different partition* — one whose tail DMA narrows. The incumbent's does not. **I believe the source reading**, because this is not a contested measurement: `_fetch_bkv` clamps the page index and issues a fixed `page_size_per_kv_packing` copy (`:177-194`), so there is no mechanism by which the tail transfer could shrink. The reference's 0.65 must not be used to discount constraint 4's 15%.
3. **VMEM ceiling.** No textual contradiction, but the incumbent's `DEFAULT_VMEM_LIMIT_BYTES = 100 MiB` exceeds the 64 MiB the reference establishes as physical. **I believe the reference**: 100 MiB is effectively "unbounded", not a budget, and the prefill path's 32 MiB einsum intermediate is already over the *default* limit — exactly the situation the reference warns leaves no headroom.
4. **Idiom taxonomy (a gap, not a contradiction).** The reference names three idioms; the incumbent is a hybrid it does not list — `PrefetchScalarGridSpec` (idiom 1) with every operand in `pltpu.HBM` and hand-rolled DMA (idiom 3). The scalar-prefetch machinery is used for the *metadata*, not for `index_map`s, since there are no `BlockSpec`s to index. A designer reading the file alone would not find this shape, and it is the right one for a kernel whose access pattern is a page table.
5. **Not a contradiction between these two, but carried forward from the brief:** `references/hardware-facts.md`'s claim that no SparseCore path exists on this stack is **wrong** — `measured (j-0b008512-36f2-4eb2-bdc6)`, 2 cores × 16 subcores, all four shapes exact. And `sparsecore_topk.py`'s docstring says "all 32 subcores on the chip" while the kernel reads `num_cores=2, num_subcores=16` and hardcodes `num_subcores = 16` at `:85` — 32 only if multiplied. The code is right; both prose statements are loose. Any candidate that reasons about subcore counts should read `sc.num_cores * sc.num_subcores`.

## Correction after §6.3

**Written after the sheet above, on a `report.md` §6.3 that did not exist when sections A–D were composed. Where this section and constraint 5 disagree, this section is what is true.** I have not re-read the report; the numbers below were handed to me by the coordinator and I record them as given.

**What changed.** Constraint 5 prices the score-buffer width via the 5.9× stage-B figure from `j-c13c8436-ee1e-49ee-b514`. That comparison moved page size 1024→256 alongside `max_model_len`, and is confounded. Job **`j-63b9f2e4-26c9-4e3a-99ce`** has since varied `max_model_len` in isolation at fixed page size 1024, and the width effect is far smaller than 5.9× at the phase that matters:

| | 16× score buffer costs, in stage B | `measured (j-63b9f2e4-26c9-4e3a-99ce)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| decode b8 | **1.77×** | |
| decode b32 | **1.17×** | |
| decode b64 — the nightly shape | **1.16×** | |
| prefill | 2.88–3.41× | |

And stage B fits **neither** buffer width nor row count: one row of 262144 costs **0.340 ms** while sixteen rows of the same width cost **0.254–0.269 ms** — more work, less time.

**Three consequences.**

1. **Stage B's width elasticity at nightly decode is ~0.05**, not ~0.55. A 16× width change moving cost 1.16× implies `log(1.16)/log(16) ≈ 0.054`. So the `bkv_p=3` score-narrowing of 10240→9216 (a 0.90× width) is worth roughly **0.6% of stage B**, and stage B is ~30% of the nightly decode call — **≈0.2% of the call.** Constraint 5's first bullet is, at nightly decode, noise. It is *not* noise at prefill (2.88–3.41× for 16×, elasticity ≈0.40), so a prefill design may still weigh it.
2. **Cost falling as rows rise is the subcore wave structure showing up as a measurement, and it corroborates constraint 14.** b=1 occupies 1 of 32 subcores, b=16 occupies 16; `num_waves = cdiv(b,32)`, so per-row cost should fall until b=32 and then step. `modelled` interpretation of the given numbers, consistent with `source (sparsecore_topk.py:148, :303, :360-377)`. Two things follow for the designer. Stage B at the nightly b=64 is already on the flat part of that curve with 0% subcore idle, so **there is no stage-B partitioning lever at the baseline of record** — which strengthens constraint 14 rather than weakening it. And any candidate that *reduces the token count* fed to stage B (e.g. splitting the batch across calls) will make stage B slower per row, not faster.
3. **The report's step-2 probe "can stage B's row width be bounded by `seq_len` instead of `max_model_len`?" is demoted.** Constraint 15 already established that the scan is `row_lengths`-bounded and only the HBM read is full-width; §6.3 now says the full-width read is worth ≈1.16× across a 16× range at the nightly decode shape. Bounding it is a small win, not the lever finding (1) made it look like. I would not spend a design on it.

**Does `bkv_p=3` survive?** **Yes, and it stays the first thing to measure — but the reason is now entirely stage A, and the expected size is smaller.** Re-stating the three halves of the original argument:

- ~~score buffer 10240→9216 narrows stage B~~ — **now ≈0.2% of the call.** Effectively dead at decode.
- **`num_bkv` 5→3 survives untouched.** 40% fewer inner iterations per grid step: 40% fewer score write-back DMAs and semaphore round trips, 40% fewer masked einsum invocations. `modelled` from `source (:276, :418)`; nothing in §6.3 bears on it.
- **KV bytes 167.8→151.0 MB survives untouched.** −10% on the term that is 95% of decode HBM traffic, in a phase the roofline calls HBM-bound (modelled floor 47.5 µs). At the measured 2.97e12 B/s that is ≈5.7 µs off the modelled floor, ≈2.6% of the 221.4 µs measured call. `modelled`.

So the recommendation holds on 2 of 3, and its expected value drops from "10% on both stages" to **≈2.5–3% of the decode call, all of it in stage A.** It stays ranked first *on cost-effectiveness rather than on size*: it is a one-tuple change at the call site (`num_kv_pages_per_block=(2,2,2)` → `(3,2,2)`, or `(3,3,3)`), needs no kernel code, and — the original reason — it must be measured before any hand-written variant, or the variant's win will be measured against a baseline that was leaving it on the table.

**Re-ranked area-2 candidates for the nightly decode baseline of record**, largest first, all `modelled`:

| | recovers | code cost | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **predicate the tail bkv block** (constraint 2/3/4) | 15% of 167.8 MB ≈ **25 MB** | real: a length-predicated DMA in `_fetch_bkv`, machinery already present in `_fetch_bq` | **largest area-2 lever** |
| **`bkv_p=3`** | ≈**17 MB** + 40% of the inner-loop overhead | **zero** — one tuple | measure first regardless |
| elide the 2 empty launches (constraint 11/12) | ≈223 µs/step *at AgentX rates* | moderate | µs unverified at nightly; the *count* is not |
| sort/group by length (constraint 19) | unknown — needs the served length distribution | moderate + 3 extra DMA waits | still un-sizeable; no artifact |
| swap the prefill `fori_loop`s (constraint 9) | 12% of prefill traffic | moderate | prefill is `unresolved`; do not spend here yet |

**These two are substitutes, not additives, and the sheet above did not say so.** At `bkv_p=3` the per-slot computed width is 3×3072 = 9216 against 8704 useful, so the rounding waste falls 15.0% → **5.6%**; predicating the tail *on top of* `bkv_p=3` recovers only the remaining ≈8 MB, not another 25. Together they reach 142.6 MB, the useful floor. A designer stacking both should quote 142.6 MB, not 167.8 − 25 − 17.
